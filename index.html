Engineering boundary

Live mode loops a chirp, locks onto the strongest periodic arrival (presumed to be direct speaker→mic leakage), and turns each chirp into a range profile: Hamming-weighted matched filter → analytic envelope → normalised to the direct arrival → averaged → optional baseline subtraction → median CFAR detection. Range is (delay × c + spacing) / 2 with c from air temperature. A wide phone beam off a smooth wall mostly returns the nearest perpendicular point, not the point along the aim line, and the true radiation axis of your speaker is unknown to the app. Laptop stereo mode instead triangulates from two speakers: left speaker up-chirp and right speaker down-chirp on alternate periods; it gives distance and left–right angle only (about 5° per 2 cm of range error with a 28 cm baseline). Up/down and front/back cannot be separated with three transducers in a line, and reflectors at nearly equal path lengths merge. Speaker/mic high-frequency response, OS audio processing, multipath, hand motion and untracked phone translation can all invalidate distances. Research prototype — not a calibrated depth sensor, SLAM, or clinical ultrasound. The wireframe links nearby angular samples for illustration only.
