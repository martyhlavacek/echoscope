  1. Hold the phone in front of you, screen facing you, upright — the back camera looks into the room.
  2. Press Start, hold still a moment, then turn slowly on the spot through a full circle (about 30 s). Each tick is a photo.

Engineering boundary

Live mode loops a chirp, locks onto the strongest periodic arrival (presumed to be direct speaker→mic leakage), and turns each chirp into a range profile: Hamming-weighted matched filter → analytic envelope → normalised to the direct arrival → averaged → optional baseline subtraction → median CFAR detection. Range is (delay × c + spacing) / 2 with c from air temperature. A wide phone beam off a smooth wall mostly returns the nearest perpendicular point, not the point along the aim line, and the true radiation axis of your speaker is unknown to the app. Laptop stereo mode instead triangulates from two speakers: left speaker up-chirp and right speaker down-chirp on alternate periods; it gives distance and left–right angle only (about 5° per 2 cm of range error with a 28 cm baseline). Up/down and front/back cannot be separated with three transducers in a line, and reflectors at nearly equal path lengths merge. Speaker/mic high-frequency response, OS audio processing, multipath, hand motion and untracked phone translation can all invalidate distances. Research prototype — not a calibrated depth sensor, SLAM, or clinical ultrasound. The wireframe links nearby angular samples for illustration only.

Room capture. A single photo gives only relative depth: the AI model (Depth Anything V2) knows what is nearer and farther but not by how much in metres. Each photo's depth is therefore calibrated against the floor, which is the phone height you enter below the camera, and against the photos it overlaps; any error in that height scales the whole room. Mirrors, windows, glossy or blank walls, dim light and very large rooms (beyond about 8 m) can mislead the depth model, and furniture is painted onto the walls and floor rather than modelled. Spots are placed by matching the walls they see, so neighbouring spots should share at least two walls.

Runs entirely in this page. No recordings or scan data are uploaded. Microphone, camera and motion sensors need HTTPS, localhost or a local file, and do not work inside sandboxed previews. Room capture downloads the AI depth model (Depth Anything V2 small, Apache-2.0) from Hugging Face and its runtime (onnxruntime-web) from jsDelivr once; photos are processed on the device. Simulation mode never opens the microphone or camera.
