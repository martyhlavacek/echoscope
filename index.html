How to capture
  1. Hold the phone in front of you, upright, screen facing you.
  2. Tap Start, hold still, then turn slowly on the spot through a full circle. Each tick is a photo.
  3. At the chime tilt down about 45° (the floor should reach the top half of the screen — not straight down at your feet) and turn around again, then look up at the ceiling in a few directions.
  4. Walk 2–3 m, tap Next spot and repeat (2–3 spots for most rooms), then tap Build room.
  5. Pick spots with open floor around you — a metre or more from counters, furniture and walls, so the photos show the floor meeting the walls in most directions — and keep people out of the photos.

How it works and its limits

   
Depth. An AI model (Depth Anything V2 small, Apache-2.0) runs in the page with onnxruntime-web, on the GPU when it can. For each photo it tells which things are nearer and farther — but not by how much in metres, and that unknown scale is different for every photo.

   
Scale. In the photos aimed down, the floor has to be a flat plane exactly your phone height below the camera; overlapping photos have to agree with each other. Solving all of that together gives every photo its scale. A wrong phone height scales the whole room: 10 cm off is about 8%.

   
Model. Spots are placed by matching the walls they see; the floor plan is the space each spot sees up to its walls (measured just under the ceiling, so furniture doesn’t dent it), optionally straightened. The photos are stitched onto walls, floor and ceiling, each part from the spot that sees it best.

   
Limits. Mirrors, windows, glossy or blank walls, dim light and very large rooms can mislead the depth model. Furniture is painted onto the walls and floor rather than modelled. Neighbouring spots should share at least two walls.
